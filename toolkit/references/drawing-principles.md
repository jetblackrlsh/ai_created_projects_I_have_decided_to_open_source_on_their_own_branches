# Applied construction decisions

- Build depth before decoration: boxes, spheres and cylinders become useful props only when their shared camera and hidden-side construction remain coherent.
- Treat the horizon as camera eye level. An independently rotated chair shares the horizon but has different horizontal vanishing directions. Equal world intervals do not produce equal screen intervals.
- Establish gesture first. The ribcage and pelvis can tilt differently; use support, negative spaces and limb overlap to distinguish anticipation, effort and recovery. Anatomy diagrams are not finished naturalistic anatomy.
- Heroic elongated adult proportions are an optional genre convention; body-size variation and child proportions remain necessary. Dramatic stance is not equivalent to indiscriminately enlarging muscles.
- Project real 3D blocks/cylinders for foreshortening studies. Authored 2D contour figures are not a continuous articulated 3D rig, and changing a head view does not rotate the entire body.
- Build a head from skull volume, jaw, center line and feature bands. Preserve age and identity across views; check the far eye against the silhouette, not just a remembered coordinate.
- Compose major masses before detail. Alternate establishment, action, reaction and detail deliberately. The eye should find the focal point without reading labels.
- Inking clarifies form and grouping. Use broad dark masses with local feathering and controlled white space, not uniform outlines everywhere or indiscriminate hatching.
- Cover thumbnails need title-safe space, focal hierarchy and readable silhouette at small scale before embellishment.

# Engineering and knowledge gaps

The source does not supply SVG algorithms, JSON schemas, render tests, digital patch proofs, general joint rigs, detailed hand/foot anatomy or a cloth solver. Those are our engineering choices or extra drawing problems, not lessons to attribute to the book. Tools support inspectable construction; they do not prove artistic mastery or alter model weights.
