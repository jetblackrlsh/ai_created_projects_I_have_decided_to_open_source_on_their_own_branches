# Audio production

Read runtime-setup.md, audio-runtime-manifest.json, audio-runtime-requirements.txt, vn_spec_schema.md and audio-mixing.md in this directory. Use the bundled scripts/setup_audio_runtime.py and adapters. Obtain the current user’s authorization before downloading required external models/runtime and check applicable model terms. A prior private user’s permission is not a grant to other users. No weights are bundled.

Assign narrator and every speaking character a distinct stable Kokoro voice. Use language-compatible stock voices and verify availability before recording all lines. Keep casting in a JSON mapping and labels in the transcript. Do not clone a real person's voice. Normalize measured speech, preserve utterance boundaries, and keep separate voice, effects and music stems.

Use continuous musical phrases across coherent scenes rather than restarting a tiny loop for each panel. Generate distinct motivated effects synchronized with the action. Document intentionally quiet panels. Check generation output, duration, nonzero amplitude and full decode. Stable Audio silence/failure is not equivalent to an intentional silent passage.

## Audible music, not merely lower music

Keep voices > foreground SFX > music, while music remains clearly audible at normal listening volume. Judge this hierarchy during meaningful active phrases/events, not at every sample: a silent voice or fading effect cannot literally remain louder than continuous music. Never apply a new music attenuation independently for each quiet syllable or effect tail. Do not use a global track-average ratio as proof of audibility.

Normalize speech consistently first. Start with active speech near -18 dBFS RMS, foreground SFX near -24, music near -29 during speech and -25 in gaps. These are calibration starting points, not perceptual guarantees or LUFS measurements. Use phrase-level envelopes with smooth attack/release and bounded ducking (normally no more than 4 dB from the established bed). Restore the bed after phrases. Avoid stacking duckers. Music should normally sit 8–14 dB below active speech, and at least 3 dB below meaningful foreground SFX. Preserve peak headroom (master true peak <= -1 dBTP).

Measure post-gain stems over 3-second windows and every utterance/effect interval, not only the whole film. Set a declared music presence floor (default -33 dBFS RMS in ordinary scored windows) and fail sustained undershoots; explicitly list brief planned fades/silences with time and narrative reason. A source rest, weak generation, limiter collapse, or excessive ducking is not automatically an intentional exception. Repair it with better cue selection, editing/overlap, moderate phrase leveling or a regenerated cue. Do not amplify true silence or noise arbitrarily to satisfy a number. If the audible floor and foreground hierarchy conflict, correct the foreground normalization/arrangement rather than simply suppressing music. Audit after master scaling and encoding too.

Run `scripts/check_music_presence.py final-scaled-music.wav --output music-presence.json` on the music stem after ALL common master gain/headroom scaling. This gate must pass; it does not replace separate active-foreground hierarchy and listening checks. A single music transient can force a large common attenuation: repair or regenerate that cue, then recheck the final-scaled floor instead of validating only the pre-master bed.

Record window-level distributions, failing timestamps, thresholds, exceptions and the stem gain changes. Fail closed on missing/nonfinite measurements. Listen to a mixed preview covering narration, a mixed-speaker caption, a loud effect and a quiet scene when a real listening surface is available. Compare at the same playback volume. Quantitative checks support but cannot certify perceived audibility. If listening cannot be performed, disclose only that limitation; do not ship a known failed presence floor as a completed fix.

Use the backend's source placement, canonical-master reconstruction and encoded-boundary timing checks with its negative controls. Do not remove audible music merely to increase isolated-speech correlation. Diagnose speech prominence separately from timing. Listen to the final encoded mix when possible; otherwise explicitly mark listening unverified. Preserve stems for a targeted rebalance instead of regenerating accepted speech.

## AI pronunciation and cache safety

Keep displayed captions exactly as supplied. For English Kokoro speech, use `scripts/kokoro_pronunciation.py` to phonemize uppercase AI as /ˌeɪˈaɪ/ (the letter A followed by eye). Never substitute `A I` or `ay eye` as plain text: the pinned kokoro-onnx/espeak runtime reads the former as /ɐ ˈaɪ/ and the latter as /ˈaɪ ˈaɪ/. Misaki markup is not supported by this backend.

Call `phonemize_for_kokoro(spoken_text, engine.tokenizer, lang)` and pass the returned phonemes to `engine.create(..., is_phonemes=True)`. Keep them separate from source/caption text. Apply at the actual per-page synthesis call, not only a top-level `tts_text` variable that pagination may ignore. Record pronunciation version and phonemes in provenance; invalidate old audio caches when pronunciation changes, then rebuild measured timings and the final mix. Do not reuse stale WAVs solely because caption/voice are unchanged. Inspect intended phonemes and listen to a preview when possible; phoneme and waveform tests alone do not certify perception. This helper deliberately does not guess other acronyms, names or lowercase words; establish and test those separately.
