# Create Eight-Page Comic Spreads

## Plan the comic

1. Default to `create-jet-paint-template-art`, not the modern-anime `generate-jet-style-images` aesthetic. Read that skill and its reference guide, then inspect the relevant approved original templates before generation. Use original characters for a new original story unless the user specifies a cast. Honor an explicitly requested alternate style instead of forcing this default; keep the story-clarity and caption requirements in either style.
2. Honor the requested premise, length, audience, and supplied reference format. Default to a complete eight-page original story for an unspecified test. Give it an opening, developing conflict, decisive action, and satisfying ending. Do not replace a complete story with a teaser.
3. Divide the story into ordered batches of eight pages. For N pages, create ceiling(N/8) spread images. Never put more than eight pages in one image. Keep the final batch at its actual page count: 9 pages means 8+1; 16 means 8+8; 18 means 8+8+2. Maintain continuous page numbering across images.
4. Treat a page as a distinct bordered portrait page, not merely one panel in an eight-panel comic. Default to two simple panels per page, but use a deliberate full-page splash when the story benefits. Keep panel counts restrained to protect lettering size.
5. For eight pages, use a flat landscape contact sheet of four columns by two rows, pages 1–4 above 5–8. Use clean, generous gutters separating pages and thinner dividers inside each page. Adapt smaller final batches without blank or duplicate filler pages. Follow an explicit user reference layout instead when provided. Never simulate a photographed book or add perspective distortion.
6. Write fuller, longer captions in plain, complete sentences, plus a visual beat for each panel. Do not default to terse 4–10-word fragments. Usually give each page two to four purposeful sentences (roughly 30–60 words across its caption boxes), adjusting to the story and readable space rather than padding to a quota. Explicit shorter-text requests and verbatim supplied text take precedence. Explain who is acting, what they want, why they act, what changes, and how that change leads to the next beat. Keep dialogue attributed and avoid flowery phrasing or unexplained invented terms.
7. Reserve sufficient caption space BEFORE generation. Prefer one large illustrated scene or fewer panels when fuller narration needs room; use multiple well-padded caption boxes when helpful. Never solve overflow with tiny lettering, clipped text, or deletion of essential context. If a longer comic is permitted, distribute beats across additional pages/spreads; if exactly eight pages are requested, simplify the plot and composition while preserving the causal links.
8. Build a cast-to-template plan before prompting. Assign EVERY character, including allies, villains, bystanders and background figures, the closest approved base and note the adaptations. Robots, humans, ghosts, animals and monsters must share the same template-derived visual grammar, not just the protagonist. A single template-styled hero surrounded by generic cartoon/anime people fails the style requirement. Group similar incidental figures under an explicit shared base; for unusual anatomy, adapt the nearest base and explain the adaptation rather than abandoning the style.
9. Treat each base as an unfinished construction drawing: add appropriate hair/head details, complete limbs/hands/feet/tails/wings, clothing and accessories, and finished flat colors. Missing limbs, tail stubs, exposed blank scalp and monochrome are not default stylistic features. Preserve intentional subject anatomy and occlusion. Record each finished design's proportions, colors, clothing and props and keep them consistent across every page.

## Review the story before generating

Read the captions in order as if the reader knows nothing about the premise. Verify that every important person, place, object, goal and ability is introduced before it matters; motivation is stated; actions have understandable consequences; transitions follow from those consequences; and the ending resolves the established problem. Explain necessary travel, time changes, discoveries and changes of allegiance. Do not give a character knowledge or an objective the story never established. Check the visual beats against this same causal chain. Revise any confusing jump before spending an image generation. Longer captions must add context, not repeated information or decorative prose.

## Reference handling

Use the selected original bases as actual image references, not only the words “MS Paint style.” Use the companion skill's approved views and, for a known person/avatar, their authorized private identity sheet alongside the base. Record which reference anchors each cast member. If reference slots are limited, prioritize representative bases covering the cast and include explicit character-to-base mappings in the prompt. Do not replace original anchors with generic art or an earlier generated scene merely for convenience. If the template pack is unavailable, retrieve the authorized companion skill/reference pack or ask for it; do not claim template fidelity from text alone.

The public companion skill is on branch `create-jet-paint-template-art` of `jetblackrlsh/ai_created_projects_I_have_decided_to_open_source_on_their_own_branches`. Personal portraits are optional private references, not part of this public comic skill. Never publish them with skill copies without separate authorization.

## Generate one whole spread

1. Use the built-in image-generation tool to generate every page in the batch together as ONE image in ONE generation. Do not generate pages separately and stitch them together. Do not substitute HTML, SVG, a PDF, or code-drawn art for the image.
2. Request the largest supported landscape image appropriate to the page grid. Do not claim a pixel size that the tool does not provide. A dense eight-page image may need zooming on a phone; prioritize large lettering and disclose any remaining readability limit.
3. Put these requirements explicitly in every self-contained image prompt:
   - Default: finished, fully colored artwork in the supplied Jet MS Paint templates' visual language: broad stylized heads, oversized nested eyes, tiny economical facial marks, simple template-derived bodies, slightly irregular pixel-stepped black contours and flat closed color shapes. State the selected base and completed features for EVERY character, including supporting cast and background figures. No generic anime/realistic supporting cast, monochrome template output or unfinished stubs. Create cinematic drama through framing, staging, scale, color contrast and action, not a switch to glossy anime rendering, gradients or photorealism. Use a different visual language only when explicitly requested.
   - Exact number of distinct portrait pages, grid positions, page numbers, and unambiguous left-to-right then top-to-bottom reading order. Put page labels and any title in rectangular caption boxes too.
   - NO speech bubbles and NO thought balloons. Put ALL story text, dialogue, thoughts, narration, and any sound-effect words inside rectangular caption boxes with no tails. Prefer visual effects over sound-effect words.
   - Large, heavy bold, clear sans-serif lettering. High-contrast text on solid caption backgrounds; ample padding; no tiny print, script fonts, clipped text, decorative text, or lettering over busy art. Preserve exact wording and spelling. Attribute dialogue or thoughts by name when needed.
   - Full character and location descriptions, exact caption strings, and the action for every panel on every page. No extra characters, limbs, unsupported props, duplicate pages, watermark, or unrequested text.
4. End reusable or external prompts with `LANDSCAPE ASPECT RATIO` when using the default spread grid. Honor explicit orientation requests.
5. For later spreads, restate the complete visual descriptions and continuity state. If supported, use an inspected accepted earlier spread as a visual reference, not as a spot-edit target.

## Inspect and regenerate

1. Visually inspect EVERY generated spread, both as a whole and zoomed to each page. Read every caption and compare with the planned text. Inspection-only crops or zooms are acceptable; do not modify the deliverable.
2. Check page count, order, page boundaries, internal panel flow, story completeness, continuity, repeated or omitted beats, anatomy, hands, faces, clothing, props, architecture, unintended objects, caption spelling, bold font size, contrast, clipping, and the total absence of speech/thought balloons. Re-read the actual rendered captions as a first-time reader: motivations, discoveries, consequences and transitions must remain explicit and understandable. Check EVERY visible character against the cast-to-template plan, including small supporting figures; verify finished color, hair and intended anatomy, consistent proportions and no unintended stubs. Reject mixed-style casts even when the hero looks correct.
3. If ANY issue is found, regenerate the ENTIRE affected spread from its complete prompt with explicit corrective constraints. Do not perform spot edits, inpainting, caption overlays, panel replacements, or pasted fixes. Keep unaffected accepted spreads intact.
4. Reinspect every replacement with the same checks. Never describe an uninspected result as checked, perfect, or fixed. After repeated failure, simplify the composition or split/restructure caption boxes first. Tighten redundant words only while retaining full context and causal links; do not revert to cryptic fragments. Regenerate again. If a tool limit prevents an acceptable result, report the exact unresolved limit rather than silently delivering it as final.

## Deliver

Attach every accepted full-resolution spread image in story order, labeled with its page range. Use native file attachments where available. Include a concise title and note any real readability limitations. Provide a PDF or separate page exports only when requested; never replace the primary spread images with those extras. Do not publish externally or add unrelated backups unless requested or independently authorized.
